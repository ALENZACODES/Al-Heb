# Word Fix, 16 November 2026

A full hearing on a GPU (13,812 word, builder and dictionary recordings, each heard once) flagged 599 recordings: `docs/audio/gpu-flagged-2026-11-16.json`.

Each flagged recording then went through `tools/audio/asr-check/fix_list.py` with the voice key:

1. The clip as it stood was heard twice. Heard right both times: left alone.
2. Otherwise new takes were recorded until one was heard right twice, and that take replaced the clip.
3. Every replacement was then heard by the sound model (`sound_gate.py`), because the word check compares letters and cannot hear a wrong vowel. A replacement with fewer than 75% of its sounds heard was sent back and the earlier clip put in place again.

| outcome | recordings |
|---|---|
| heard right twice as it stood, left alone | 69 |
| replaced with a new take (passed both checks) | 239 |
| replaced, then sent back by the sound check (earlier clip in place) | 178 |
| no take heard right twice, left as it was (listed in `ASR-NEEDS-HUMAN.md`) | 75 |
| replaced from an out-of-date text, put back (i-16-w8, both voices: `audio/INDEX.csv` still says מִטְעָן, the lesson teaches מַטְעֵן) | 2 |
| not reached (two slices ran out of time) | 36 |

What the sound check showed: of the takes read from pointed text in the lesson voice almost all were kept; of the takes read from unpointed text more than half had the wrong vowels (שָׁקַט read "sheket", אָרַג read "ereg"). A take read without vowel points should not be trusted on the word check alone.

Logs: `docs/audio/word-fix/r2-*.jsonl` (every take), `tools/audio/asr-check/sound-gate-log.jsonl` (the sound check).
A machine process from end to end. The 253 recordings in the third and fourth rows (178 + 75) are the ones for a teacher's ear or a human voice.

## Second Opinion On The 253 Left In Place

The 253 clips the word check still does not pass (178 sent back + 75 with no passing take) were heard by the sound model as they stand (`sound_second_opinion.py`, table in `docs/audio/word-fix/second-opinion.csv`):

| sound model | clips |
|---|---|
| sounds match the pointed word (85% or more) | 210 |
| close (75% to 85%) | 16 |
| neither model hears the word | 27 |

So most of these are words the word model does not know (rare, biblical or Aramaic words come back as a commoner word), not wrong audio. The 27 in the last row, and then the 16, are the ones to play by ear first.
