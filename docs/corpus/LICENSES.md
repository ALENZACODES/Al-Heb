## 4. Normalisation applied to every Hebrew string

Documented in the header of `src/corpus/normalize.js` and enforced by tests:
1. Pointed text, **cantillation removed** (U+0591-05AF), plus meteg U+05BD, rafe U+05BF, paseq U+05C0, U+05C4/5C5, CGJ and zero-width characters. The raw cantillated source strings are kept in `data/corpus/source-snapshot.json`.
2. Metsudah holam-haser U+05BA becomes ordinary holam U+05B9.
3. The Tetragrammaton is printed in the UXLC form (yod+sheva, he, vav+qamats, he); siddur sources that print it with a holam are rewritten to this form. Readers say "Adonai" (the audio text sent to TTS is the spoken substitute).
4. ASCII colon becomes sof pasuq U+05C3; commas, full stops (except at the end), and parentheses are dropped; a space after maqaf is removed; Ten-Days-of-Repentance variants in parentheses are omitted.
5. Final step `normalize('NFC')`. For Hebrew this does not compose characters; it applies the canonical order of combining marks (vowel, then dagesh, then shin/sin dot) so identical words are identical strings.

Clipped records (documented in each record's `notes`): `bless-formula-1` (first six words of HaMotzi), `num-12-13` (last five words of Num 12:13), `avot-1` (cut before the closing clause, whose divine name is deliberately scrambled in the source), `birkat-hamazon-1` (the opening only), `modeh-ani-1` (first sentence).

## 5. Items still unverified or open

- Every Sefaria licence label above (Torat Emet, Shaliehsaboo, Metsudah, JPS 1917) is Sefaria's own; Sefaria's terms of use and per-text licence pages were not read this session.
- No Hebrew educator has proofread any pointed text. Pointing in the siddur items is as the source has it (for example the source line for Modeh Ani in the CC0 edition lacks a shin dot; we used the Metsudah line instead).
- Aramaic/Hebrew authorship and dating statements about Kaddish, Aleinu, Adon Olam and the Amidah are not made anywhere in the data.
- The feminine form of Modeh Ani is derived (modeh to modah), not fetched.
- Nusach: siddur texts come from Ashkenaz (Metsudah), Sefard (Torat Emet) and Edot HaMizrach editions. Wording differs slightly between rites (notably Aleinu); `traditionNeutral: true` refers to the Hebrew letters being the same for a learner, not to every rite using this exact wording.
- The English glosses are literal and original; they were not reviewed by a Hebrew educator.
- English of JPS 1917 is reproduced as Sefaria serves it (including capitalised "HEAR, O ISRAEL" in Deut 6:4).
- Phrasebook: pointing, register, slang, gender forms and transliteration all need native-speaker review.
- KJV and UK Crown copyright, Sefaria API terms of use, and the licences of eyaler/hebrew_wordlists, Hebrew WordNet, HF hebrew-words, Hadshon, Harpatka/Shabes stories and Chabad audio were not checked (they are not used).
