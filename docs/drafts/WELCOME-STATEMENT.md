# Welcome statement (draft for John to approve; not yet in the app)

## Short version (for the Home "About" line and the credits page)

ALEF is free for everyone who wants to learn Hebrew. People of every background are welcome here: any race, culture, faith or no faith, any caste or class, any gender or sexual orientation, any family shape, any ability, any age.

## Longer version (for the About page)

ALEF is a free place to learn Hebrew. Anyone who wants to read Hebrew, pray in it, travel with it, or simply learn it is welcome to start today. You do not need to share a faith, a heritage, a family background or an opinion with the person who made this app. Learners of every race, culture, caste, class, gender, sexual orientation, family shape, ability and age are welcome, and so are people who are Jewish, people who are not, people who are exploring, and people who are returning.

The app teaches letters, sounds and words. It does not ask who you are, and it does not ask you to prove anything. We teach three ways of pronouncing Hebrew (Israeli, Sephardi and Ashkenazi) so you can choose the one your community or your plans need.

If something in ALEF excludes you or hurts you, please tell us so we can fix it.

## Notes for John (not for the app)

- This is a welcome-to-learn statement. It makes no claim about religious law, doctrine or who belongs to what community, which is why it should sit comfortably with Reform, and is also unlikely to be a problem for other movements. A statement that took positions on doctrine would be different.
- Reform Judaism publicly affirms LGBTQ+ people, Jews of colour, interfaith families and people with disabilities (sources: reformjudaism.org "What is Reform Judaism", and the Union for Reform Judaism's pages on LGBTQ+ affirmation and interfaith inclusion). A broad welcome like this one matches that stance. I have not asked any rabbi. Please show the text to one Reform rabbi, and one rabbi from another movement if you like, before you publish. That is the only real check.
- "Caste" is in the list on purpose, as you asked. It is not a common word in Hebrew-learning apps, so keep it if the audience you picture includes South Asian learners; remove it if it looks odd to your reviewers.
- The last sentence ("tell us so we can fix it") needs a real way to report a problem. Do not publish it until the app has one.
- The accessibility lanes are writing a separate accessibility statement. The two should link to each other.
