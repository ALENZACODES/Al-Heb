# ALEF facilitator guide: "Read Hebrew" cohort (5 x 90 minutes)

**DRAFT. Not reviewed by clergy or educators.** All wording is ALEF's own. Hebrew text is never typed here: every text is named by its ALEF corpus id and read from the app. Lesson ids (r01 and so on) are the real ids in `data/curriculum/lessons.json`.

Who this is for: adults who cannot yet read Hebrew letters, in a congregation, JCC or campus group. Leader: someone who can read pointed Hebrew aloud. Learners use ALEF free on their own phones (no accounts, no fees).

What a 5-week cohort can honestly do: five sessions is a short start. By the end most learners can name the letters taught, sound out simple pointed words slowly, and sound out a few short prayer lines with help. Fluency needs weeks of practice after. The 10-week option (end of this guide) covers more ground.

## Before session 1
- Set the room tradition (see `settings-sheet.md`) and tell learners which one you chose and why.
- Open ALEF once on the room Wi-Fi so it can save itself for offline use. Check on two or three devices.
- Print or write the pre/post check lines (below). Decide who sits where for the access notes.

## Every session: shape (90 minutes)
| Min | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0-10 | Settle and warm-up | Finger on the right edge, move left. Re-say last week's letters. Ask who practised. |
| 10-35 | New letters and signs | Show, say, echo. Name then sound. Trace in the air. Learners follow the lesson in ALEF at their own pace. |
| 35-45 | Break | Water, stretch, phones down. |
| 45-70 | Decode drill | Syllable, then word, chorus then one at a time. Mix in last week's letters. |
| 70-82 | Read-aloud text | The named text from the app, line by line, then once straight through. |
| 82-90 | Close | One thing learned, one question parked, home practice. No one is called on without a choice to pass. |

Useful leader phrases: "Move your finger from the right." "Say the name, then the sound." "Slow is fine. Read it again." "Which sign is under that letter?"

## Pre/post read-aloud check
Purpose: a private, kind snapshot, not a test.
- Texts (open each in ALEF's reader by id): `num-12-13` (a five-word line) and `shema-1` (a short line, six words).
- Session 1, at the start: each learner reads aloud to the leader one at a time or in pairs, away from the group. Expect little. For each word mark: read correctly, read with a hint, not read. Write the totals on the learner's card, not on a board.
- Session 5, at the end: the same two texts, same method. Total the words read correctly without a hint and compare.
- Say plainly: marking is by hand and by ear. It is a guide, not a validated score. A learner may decline; record "declined", never "failed".
- Note which tradition you were listening for (the reading must match the room preset).
- Record results on the feedback form, Part B.

## Session plans

### Session 1: First letters, first words
In the room: lessons r01 (Shin and Bet), r02 (Tav and the dot in Bet), r03 (Lamed, final Mem and Vav: Shalom).
Home before session 2: r04 (Shabbat Shalom: review), r05 (Meet the Priestly Blessing: listen and spot).
Read-aloud: `bless-priestly-1`, listen only; the leader reads it, learners follow with a finger.
Plan: pre-check as learners arrive (one learner at a time, away from the group, while the others start the first letter tracing; or run it in the break). Then the block table above. Goal by the end: say the names and sounds of Shin, Bet, Tav, tell Bet from Vet by the dot, read Shabbat and Shalom slowly right to left.
Watch for: reading left to right; mixing up Vav and Bet; Tet and Tav.

### Session 2: Vowel sounds, five new letters
In the room: r06 (Yod and the 'ee' vowel), r07 (Alef and He), r08 (Tzere: the 'ay' vowel), r09 (Resh and Mem).
Home before session 3: r10 (Kaf, final Kaf), r11 (Segol: the 'eh' vowel).
Read-aloud: `bless-priestly-1`, listen again, then echo the first word only.
Goal: sound out two-letter syllables, then short words made from the letters taught. Use the Vowel board and Letters tracing in ALEF for the drill block.
Watch for: dropping the vowel under a letter; treating Alef as a sound.

### Session 3: More vowels, the shva
In the room: r12 (Nun and final Nun), r13 (Shuruk and Kubutz: the 'oo' vowels), r14 (Shva at the start of a syllable).
Home before session 4: r15 (Priestly Blessing, line 1), r16 (Pe and final Pe), r17 (Chet and the sneaky patach).
Read-aloud: `zimun-opening`, the leader reads, then learners echo line by line (this is a stretch text this early; allow reading with help).
Goal: read short words with five vowel signs; first sight of the shva.
Watch for: silent versus sounded shva (do not teach every rule yet: "if unsure, say a very short e and keep going").

### Session 4: The rest of the letters, half vowels
In the room: r18 (Dalet and Gimel), r19 (Ayin), r20 (Samekh and Sin), r21 (Chataf vowels: the half vowels). Keep it light; letters not reached are home work.
Home before session 5: r23 (Zayin and Tet), r24 (Tsadi, final Tsadi and Qof: the alphabet is done).
Read-aloud: `num-12-13`, read in chorus, then in pairs.
Goal: confidence, not coverage. Run a "sound-gap" game in the drill block (letters that sound alike, such as Tet and Tav) using ALEF's Sound gap screen.
Watch for: fatigue and drop-off. Offer a catch-up pairing for anyone who missed a week.

### Session 5: Real lines, post-check, what next
In the room: r22 (Shema, line 1), r27 (The full Priestly Blessing, read straight through), r28 (The blessing formula).
Home after the cohort: r29 (Shva rules), r30 (Dagesh, rafe and mappiq), r48 (Timed reading 1), then the 10-week track from week 6 onward.
Read-aloud: `shema-1`, then `bless-priestly-1`, `bless-priestly-2`, `bless-priestly-3` (full blessing), each line chorus then pairs. `hamotzi` can be used as a closing blessing over bread if your community does so; check with your clergy.
Plan: post-check in the first 25 minutes; the rest follows the block table. Close with a short circle: one thing each person can now read. Hand out the feedback form (Part A).
Goal: read one pointed prayer line slowly. Do not promise fluency.

## Pronunciation questions: Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Israeli
Learners will ask "which is right?" The honest answer: all are real Hebrew traditions; the room should use one so people are not confused, and your clergy decide which.
- Say the rule once: "In this room we read it this way. At other places you will hear another way, and that is normal."
- ALEF's three settings: Israeli, Sephardi (stored value; ALEF treats the common American synagogue sound as Sephardi/Israeli vowel values), and Ashkenazi. See `settings-sheet.md`.
- Do not argue which is "more authentic". If a learner who knows another tradition corrects you, thank them and keep the room preset.
- Typical differences learners notice: how Tav without a dot sounds in some Ashkenazi readings, how kamatz and cholam are said, and where the stress falls. Keep examples to what you can model confidently.
- If you are unsure, say so and ask your rabbi, cantor or Hebrew teacher. Park the question rather than guess.
- A learner who moves between traditions: tell them to finish this cohort in the room's setting, then change the ALEF setting afterward.

## Accessibility notes
- Large print: let learners zoom the phone (ALEF does not block zoom). Offer letters on a whiteboard or projector at a large size.
- Seating: a step-free route, a seat near the board, space for a mobility device, a quiet corner. Ask at sign-up, privately.
- Vision: learners using a screen reader or high-contrast mode should say so in advance; ALEF's accessibility checks so far were automated and in a desktop browser; it has not been tested with screen readers on phones, so try the lessons with the learner before session 1 and tell them honestly what works.
- Hearing: face the room when speaking; write key words on the board; use a mic if you have one; allow phones with captions or text on screen.
- Reading differences (dyslexia and similar): allow extra time, one line at a time with a card or finger as a guide, no reading aloud on the spot.
- Pace and fatigue: the break is not optional; learners may pass at any time.
- Privacy: no one is asked to share progress. ALEF keeps progress on the learner's own device.
- Shabbat and Yom Tov: schedule cohorts away from them. ALEF is not meant for use on Shabbat; the app's Shabbat streak freeze is a convenience, not a ruling. Ask your clergy.

## Limits
- Not reviewed by clergy or educators. Hebrew liturgical choices, customs and pronunciation rules are for your clergy.
- ALEF's audio is currently the device's built-in voice; recorded human audio is not ready. The leader models the sound.
- The check is hand-marked, not a validated assessment.
