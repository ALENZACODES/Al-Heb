Aleph: Read Hebrew, together

A free reading cohort your congregation can run between classes, using learners' own phones.

What Aleph is

  • Free. No fee for learners and no fee for hosts. Hosts are asked not to charge for the cohort.
  • No accounts. Nobody signs up with Aleph. Progress stays on each learner's own device.
  • Works offline once opened and installed on a phone or tablet (tested in a desktop browser; a quick check on your own devices is part of the host checklist below).
  • Reading first. Letters, vowel signs, then real prayer lines, with the leader modelling the sound.
  • Your tradition. The leader presets Ashkenazi, Sephardi or Israeli pronunciation to match the room (see the settings sheet).

What a host does

Provides

  • A room, a time, a leader who can read Hebrew aloud
  • Sharing the sign-up with your members
  • Wi-Fi for the first ten minutes (to open Aleph once)

Does not need

  • Printing costs or per-seat fees
  • Special software or accounts
  • A paid curriculum or workbook

Aleph supplies a facilitator guide, a settings sheet and a feedback form. The leader owns the room, the pronunciation choices and any prayer or customs questions.

Two cohort options

OptionShapeAim
5-week cohortFive sessions of 90 minutes, weekly. About 20 minutes of Aleph practice at home between sessions.Letters and core vowels, then sounding out simple pointed prayer lines slowly. A short start, not full fluency.
10-week cohortTen weekly sessions of 60 minutes, following Aleph's 10-week class track. Home practice from the week sheet.The full alphabet, shva and dagesh rules, then the Shema and a Friday-night page, read slowly.

Both include a short read-aloud check in the first and last session, marked by the leader by hand, so learners and the congregation can see progress.

Host checklist

  • [ ] Leader named and has read the facilitator guide
  • [ ] Pronunciation tradition chosen (settings sheet)
  • [ ] Room has a way to project or write large letters (a whiteboard is enough)
  • [ ] Learners told to bring a phone or tablet and headphones if they have them
  • [ ] Seating and lighting check: large print, a seat near the board, a step-free route

Honest limits

  • Aleph's sound today comes from the device's built-in voice, which reads modern Israeli-style Hebrew; recorded human audio for each tradition is not ready. In your room, the leader's voice is the model.
