# Rights, provenance and review checklist for AI-generated images

NOT LEGAL ADVICE. Everything below is a summary of notes already in this repo plus caveats; items marked (unverified) were not checked in this session.

## What the repo's verification notes say (docs/verification/VERIFICATION.md items 6.01-6.06, DATA-FIXES.md B13)
- OpenAI terms of use, section 3(a): OpenAI "assigns to you all its right, title and interest in and to Output", and you may use Content "for any purpose, including commercial", conditional on compliance with the terms. This was read through a web summariser (direct fetch returned 403), and no calendar date was shown on the page. **Re-read the live terms yourself before the run and before publishing** (https://openai.com/policies/terms-of-use/), including the usage policies and the page that applies to your region: the US vs non-US terms page was NOT checked (6.02). If you generate through the API instead of ChatGPT, those are separate terms (not read here).
- The API docs say text rendering "can still struggle" (6.05): another reason for the no-text rule.
- Midjourney terms and Adobe Firefly terms were NOT verified (6.06, 6.07); no image from those tools should be mixed in without reading their terms.

## Copyrightability in the US (caveat from docs/book/IMAGE-RUN.md, not independently researched here)
Owning the output under a vendor's contract is not the same as holding a copyright. US Copyright Office practice (Thaler denial; later guidance on AI-assisted works, (unverified) details) is that purely machine-generated images are likely not protectable, and that protection can reach human contributions such as selection, arrangement and substantial edits. Practical consequences: someone may copy the raw pictures with no legal recourse in the US; what you most clearly own is the vector Hebrew overlay, the layout, the text, and your own edits. Other countries differ (unverified). Ask a lawyer before relying on exclusivity (e.g. selling the book, trademarking characters like Ari and Noa).

## Keep provenance (this is what the pipeline records)
Per image `data/images/provenance.json` stores: tool, optional model, prompt text and its SHA-256, command number, ingest date, the downloaded file's name and date, SHA-256 of the untouched original, the checks and your approve/reject decision with date. The originals (with their embedded metadata) are kept outside git. Log by hand anything the pipeline cannot see: edits you make in other software (who, what, when), re-prompts, anything you paint over. Do not edit `provenance.json` by hand to look more human.
- ChatGPT images normally carry C2PA "content credentials" metadata (unverified for your files; the ingest records a rough `c2paMarker` byte check only, not a validation). Web copies have all metadata stripped, so nothing in the published files says they are AI-made; the originals keep it. Disclosure is therefore your job: use the colophon line and the app's about/credits.
- Colophon line (from IMAGE-RUN.md): "Illustrations created with AI tools and edited by the author." Say "edited" only if you did edit; do not describe the pictures as hand-drawn. Store listings (KDP, app stores) have their own AI-disclosure questions (unverified): answer them truthfully.

## Consistency checklist (go through every sheet; "reject" and regenerate on any fail)
1. **Text inside the image**: no Hebrew, nikkud, Latin letters, numerals, or letter-like marks anywhere (signs, book spines, tablets, labels, price tags, screens, cards, shirts, tattoos). The OCR flag is only a first screen; zoom in.
2. **Style drift across batches**: ChatGPT sessions drift. Compare tiles from different days side by side: line weight (thick rounded outline), flat fill (no gradients, no photographic texture), the cream background (#F6F1E7), the five-colour palette, light from upper left, 10% margin. Review each block as a set; spot-check the first and last image of every block, and re-check after any ChatGPT model/UI change. If a batch drifts, redo the batch rather than mixing.
3. **Character consistency**: Ari (35, short dark curly hair, trimmed beard, navy T-shirt, silver chain) and Noa (32, shoulder-length wavy dark hair, terracotta blouse, gold hoops) match the approved sheets in every use: hair, skin tone, clothes, proportions. The doc budgets 15-25% regeneration for drift (unmeasured).
4. **Anatomy and objects**: hands (finger count), faces, limbs, mirrored or merged objects, impossible perspective, cutlery/ladders/animals with extra parts, objects that do not match the vocabulary word they illustrate.
5. **Mnemonic fit**: the object really echoes the letter shape it is paired with and does NOT itself look like another Hebrew letter (a confusion risk for learners). Cursive sets echo the cursive reference, not the print shape.
6. **Empty areas stay empty**: frames, position cards, blank screens/covers/price tags, because the Hebrew is overlaid later.
7. **Geometry**: size/aspect as expected (the report lists `aspect_substituted` ones: check the layout copes), transparent background where asked (icons), no fringe/halo on transparent PNGs.
8. **Cultural and religious sensitivity** (have a knowledgeable reader, ideally Jewish and Israeli, check these): any depiction of Jews, rabbis, Hasidim, Israelis or Arabs must avoid caricature and stereotype (noses, beards, hats, wealth, money motifs, clannishness); avoid costume-party "Orthodox" figures for ordinary scenes; check kippah, tallit, tefillin, Torah scroll, seder plate, menorah vs hanukkiah, mezuzah, shabbat candles and challah for correct details and respectful use (no sacred objects as props in jokes, no divine-name text, no imagery of God); check food (no pork/shellfish in kosher contexts shown as typical), gendered roles, and that Israel-track scenes (cafe, market, beach, army-age people) do not read as political statements or maps with borders. Prayer scenes should show real practice (men's and women's sections vary by community). When unsure, reject.
9. **Safety**: no real people's likenesses, no logos or brands, no children in unsuitable scenes.
10. **Approvals are human**: `approve` is a statement that YOU looked at the full-size image. Do not bulk-approve by glob without opening the sheets.
