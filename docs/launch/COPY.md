## One-paragraph description

ALEF is a free Hebrew course that runs in your browser and works offline once loaded. It teaches you to read Hebrew aloud, starting from the letters and vowel points, and then to read the lines you meet in a synagogue (Torah, prayers, the service) and to say everyday words and phrases for a trip to Israel. It has lessons, review cards, letter tracing, a vocabulary list grouped by topic, and a way to compare how the Ashkenazi, Sephardi and Israeli traditions pronounce the same line. There is no account and no ads, and your progress stays on your device. It is an early draft: there are no recorded voices yet (it uses your device's Hebrew voice if it has one), and no native speaker, teacher or clergy member has reviewed the Hebrew.

## Five bullets

- **Learn to read from zero.** 125 core lessons plus 50 intermediate lessons, with spaced-repetition review cards and letter tracing.
- **Read what is in front of you in shul.** Prayer and Torah-reading practice, including reading without vowel points, an aliyah rehearsal and the Shema and Priestly Blessing with cantillation marks (the only two texts with them so far).
- **Say it in Israel.** Word and phrase sets for cafes, getting around and daily life, with speaking drills (speech recognition depends on your browser and is not checked for Hebrew).
- **Hear how traditions differ.** Compare Ashkenazi, Sephardi and Israeli readings of a word; these are generated by spelling rules, so treat them as a study aid.
- **Free, private, works offline.** No account, no tracking, no third-party services; install it to your home screen and keep studying without a connection.

## Who it is for

- Adults who go to synagogue (any denomination) and want to follow, or lead, the reading with confidence rather than guess.
- Travellers to Israel who want to read signs and menus and manage simple spoken exchanges.
- Beginners with no Hebrew, and people who learned to sound out letters long ago and want to rebuild the skill.
- Not aimed at: fluent speakers, children (there is no child-specific design), or anyone who needs a certified text.

## What it is not yet

- **No recorded audio.** 0 of 16,710 planned clips exist. Sound comes from your device's own Hebrew voice, if it has one; that is a device voice, not a teacher, and it may be missing or poor on your phone.
- **Not reviewed.** The Hebrew (lessons, vocabulary, vowel points, transliterations, prayer texts, tradition comparisons) was assembled by programs and has not been checked by a native speaker, Hebrew teacher, rabbi or cantor. 0 of 7,039 review rows have come back. Expect mistakes. Do not rely on it for liturgical use without checking a trusted printed siddur or your own teacher.
- **Not tested with learners or on real devices.** Layout checks were done in emulation, not on actual phones, and no learner has used it.
- **Not a full course.** Trope (cantillation) is only partly covered, and the vocabulary list is machine-built and ordered by frequency, not by teaching sense.
- **Licences are not settled.** Some word data and some prayer texts come from sources whose licences are still being sorted out (`docs/legal/CONTENT-LICENCE-AUDIT.md`); this is not legal advice.
