# Reviewer instructions

Generated by `node tools/review/build-packs.mjs`. Nothing in the app has been reviewed by a native speaker, teacher or clergy yet (see STATUS.md).

## How to mark

- Read each Hebrew cell aloud before you decide.
- In the verdict column write OK (you would put your name to it), Fix (and write the right text in the correction column), or Unsure (say why in notes).
- Leave a row blank if you did not look at it. Blank means "not reviewed" and that is fine.
- Do not change the id or Hebrew columns. Corrections go in the correction column only.
- Return the file as CSV (UTF-8), not .xlsx. Keep the first row (the column names).

## Reviewer types

- `teacher`: Hebrew teacher
- `cantor`: Cantor or rabbi (liturgy and trope)
- `native`: Israeli native speaker (modern usage)

## Core lessons (r01-r51, il01-il24 word lists)

### Hebrew teacher (primary)

Judge:
- Pointing: every vowel, dagesh and shin/sin dot
- Spelling of the word
- English gloss matches this Hebrew word
- Word suits the lesson it sits in (letters and vowels taught so far)

Do not judge:
- Teaching order, lesson design or app wording
- Transliteration style (only whether it matches the sound)
- Audio (none exists)

### Cantor or rabbi (liturgy and trope) (optional)

Judge:
- Only words that are also prayer words: pointing and meaning as used in the siddur

Do not judge:
- Everyday or Israeli-street words
- Teaching order

### Israeli native speaker (modern usage) (primary)

Judge:
- Whether Israelis really say the word this way today
- Register (too formal, too slang, outdated)
- Gloss is the common meaning

Do not judge:
- Biblical or liturgical-only readings of the word
- Full pointing rules (spoken Hebrew is mostly unpointed); flag only wrong vowels you hear


## Intermediate lessons I-01 to I-50 (word lists)

### Hebrew teacher (primary)

Judge:
- Pointing and spelling
- Gloss accuracy
- Whether the three transliterations (israeli / sephardi / ashkenazi) follow from the pointing

Do not judge:
- Which tradition the app should teach
- Lesson design

### Cantor or rabbi (liturgy and trope) (primary)

Judge:
- Words from prayer, Torah and Haftarah units: pointing as in a printed siddur or chumash
- Ashkenazi and Sephardi respellings as a congregation would say them

Do not judge:
- Halacha or what a congregation should do
- Modern conversational words

### Israeli native speaker (modern usage) (optional)

Judge:
- Israeli respelling and everyday meaning of non-liturgical words

Do not judge:
- Liturgical nusach and trope
- Ashkenazi and Sephardi respellings

## Prayer and Torah lines, and the trope table

### Hebrew teacher (optional)

Judge:
- Pointing against a printed text
- English line matches the Hebrew line

Do not judge:
- Nusach choice
- Trope names and cantillation
- Theology or halacha

### Cantor or rabbi (liturgy and trope) (primary)

Judge:
- Hebrew text of each line against a printed siddur or chumash of the named nusach
- Pointing, maqaf, paseq and divine-name spelling
- Trope table names (Ashkenazi and Sephardi) and the cantillated verses
- English line is a fair literal gloss

Do not judge:
- Whether the product is "kosher" or approved
- Disputes between movements
- Licence or source attribution
- Writing new text

### Israeli native speaker (modern usage) (skip)

Judge:
- Optional: modern-Hebrew sense of the English gloss

Do not judge:
- Liturgical nusach
- Trope


## Pronunciation rules and a sample of the rule-generated respellings

### Hebrew teacher (primary)

Judge:
- The rule rows first: is each rule right for the tradition it names (vowel and consonant values, stress position)
- Sample rows: the respelling follows the pointing by those rules
- Whether the rule ids on a row are the ones that apply

Do not judge:
- Which tradition the app should offer
- The spelling system itself (only whether it conveys the sound)
- Rows outside the sample: they are produced by the same rules and are not sent for review one by one

### Cantor or rabbi (liturgy and trope) (primary)

Judge:
- Sephardi and Ashkenazi rule rows and sample rows: does a congregation of that tradition say it this way (kamatz, tav, cholam, stress, final-kamatz reduction)
- Say which community you judge by in notes

Do not judge:
- Israeli rows
- Disputes about which Ashkenazi or Sephardi sub-tradition is "right"

### Israeli native speaker (modern usage) (primary)

Judge:
- Israeli rule rows and sample rows: matches how the word is said in Israel today
- Phrasebook rows: natural everyday sound

Do not judge:
- Ashkenazi and Sephardi rows (skip unless you know the tradition)

Do not judge:

### Cantor or rabbi (liturgy and trope) (skip)

Do not judge:
- Everything in this pack

### Israeli native speaker (modern usage) (primary)

Judge:
- Would a person really say this line

- Gender of speaker matches the verb forms

Do not judge:
- Liturgical or biblical readings
- Prices and names (made-up for practice)
