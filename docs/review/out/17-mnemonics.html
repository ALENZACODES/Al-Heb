Priority 4 · 69 items · 3.3 h estimated · suggested reviewer: Hebrew teacher
How to use this printout. Memory hooks for each letter. The Hebrew shown is the example word. Check its pointing and meaning, and the claim about what the letter name means.
  • Read the Hebrew aloud. Tick ok only if you would put your name to it; fix if you can give the right text; unsure if you cannot tell.
  • The record of your answers is the matching CSV sheet (same ids), not this page. Use this page for reading and for notes on paper.
  • Items are in the order of the sheet. Please do not guess: "unsure" is a useful answer.

חָלָב
chalav
milk (vocabulary rank 1139)
Picture: A closed gate at the edge of a loch (a lake), with a jug of milk standing at the gate.
Hook: loch (final). A scrape from the throat, like the ch in loch said the Scottish way. Not lock, not the ch of chair.
Story: A closed gate by the loch: chhh! A jug of milk, chalav.
Tip: Not he: chet is closed all round; he has a gap in the left wall.
israeli: Same sound: ch as in Bach / loch (back-of-throat, no English chair sound)
sephardi: Differs: pharyngeal [ħ], a throat sound (the ch of Bach / loch is the Israeli merged value). Strict Sephardi reading uses a deeper throat sound; the loch scrape is the common Israeli value.
ashkenazi: Same sound: ch as in Bach / loch (back-of-throat, no English chair sound); the pharyngeal is seldom observed in practice
Check: Memory chain for "Chet". (1) Pointing and meaning of the word (it must START with the letter). (2) Does the picture suit the letter shape and is it fine for adults and children? (3) Does the story or the sound line say anything untrue about this letter in any of the three traditions? Points noted: Many English speakers say loch as lock; teach the scrape, not the k. No cat here: the one cat picture in the set is nun. Sephardi differs: see perTradition. Vocabulary record h35be21c5 is flagged needsReview in words.json.
