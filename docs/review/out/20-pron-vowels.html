1niqqud:kamatz בָ אָבKamatz (kamatz gadol)father
Israeli: a as in father (short, no glide)
Ashkenazi: aw as in saw (Barukh -> Bawrukh)
Sephardi: a as in father
American synagogue: ah as in father; older or Orthodox speakers say aw as in saw
Note: In Ashkenazi the alternative values are [u] in open syllables (southern dialects) and [ʌ] (Lithuanian). In standard print kamatz gadol and kamatz katan look identical; Israeli readers treat a plain kamatz as [a] except for a short memorised list (see kamatz-katan variant).		 okfix?

13niqqud:dagesh בּ שַׁבָּתDageshShabbat
Israeli: no sound of its own; makes b, k, p instead of v, kh, f
Ashkenazi: same; also makes tav t instead of s
Sephardi: same
American synagogue: same as Israeli; a tav with dagesh is always t
Note: Two things share one sign: dagesh kal (begadkefat hardening) and dagesh chazak (doubling). Dagesh chazak is acoustically and phonologically nonexistent in Modern Hebrew; it matters for reading only as a shva-na trigger and as a spelling convention. Mappiq (dagesh in a final he) means the he is pronounced.		 okfix?
