Write Hebrew by hand: cursive words

Israelis write by hand in ktav yad (cursive), which looks different from the printed letters you read. This workbook gives every one of the 27 letter forms (22 letters and 5 final forms) a handwriting row and up to three real words to copy, in alphabet order.

How to use it

  1. Look at the stroke box: the numbered circle is where a stroke starts, the arrowhead where it ends.
  2. Trace the letter row from the right: solid, then grey, then dotted, then write it alone.
  3. Each word row shows the pointed printed word (how you read it), its sound and meaning, then the handwritten word to trace. Handwriting is usually written without vowel signs, so the traced word has none.
  4. Tick the box when you can write the word without looking.
DRAFT: cursive stroke order not reviewed by a native writer; words from the course data, not teacher-checked. Cursive shapes come from the font Gveret Levin (SIL OFL 1.1); real handwriting joins and varies. Words marked * have a machine-made transliteration (Israeli, siddur style) that no native speaker has checked. Every letter form has at least one word.
