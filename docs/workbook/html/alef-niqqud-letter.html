The vowel signs (niqqud)

Hebrew letters are consonants; the vowels are small marks under, above or beside them. Prayer books, children's books and this course show the marks; newspapers and street signs do not. This workbook gives each of the 15 signs in data/pronunciation/niqqud.json a tracing row and 6 real words to copy, pronounced the Israeli way.

How to use it

  1. Say the sound in the heading, then trace the sign on the five sample letters (bet, lamed, mem, resh, tav), from the right.
  2. Copy each word: solid, then grey, then dotted, then on your own. Keep the marks small and exactly under (or above) their letter.
  3. Read the word aloud each time you write it. The transliteration is a guide, not a second spelling.
DRAFT: not reviewed by a Hebrew teacher; Israeli values only; example words from the course data, pointing not teacher-checked. Words come from the phrasebook, the lesson lists and the 5,000-word list; words marked * carry a machine-made transliteration (Israeli, siddur style) that no native speaker has checked. Sounds are Modern Israeli only: Sephardi and Ashkenazi values differ for several signs (see the pronunciation data).
