Learn to write the Hebrew letters

The letters are in teaching order, not alphabet order: Shin and Bet first, then the letters of lessons 1 to 10 of the course, then the rest. The vowel kamatz and your first syllables come right after the first nine letters. Hebrew is written and read from right to left. In every practice row, the first (black) model letter is at the right: trace it, then keep writing to the left. The letters fade from solid grey, to dotted, to empty, so you finish by writing on your own.

How to use this workbook

  1. Look at the stroke-order page at the start of each section. The numbers show the order; the arrow shows where each stroke ends.
  2. In each row, trace the grey and dotted letters, then fill the empty space.
  3. Tick the box at the left of the row when you can write that letter 3 times without looking at the model.
  4. Colour in one circle in the progress chart below each time you do that (three circles per letter).
DRAFT: stroke order not yet reviewed by a native teacher. The numbered strokes in this workbook were computed from fonts and checked only against one outside reference; if your teacher shows a different order, follow your teacher. Print stroke order is a proposal; Israeli schools may teach print letters mainly for reading.

Aleph · Print workbook · Look-alike lettersNo.   Date  

Letters that are easy to mix up. Trace a whole set in a row, then write the set from memory.

Bet / Kaf / PeBet's base sticks out to the right past the back; kaf is a single curved stroke like a reversed c; pe has a curl inside.
Gimel / NunGimel has a foot at the bottom left; nun has a flat base and no foot.
Dalet / Resh / Kaf sofitDalet has a sharp corner and its top bar sticks out past the stem; resh is one rounded curve; final kaf is a resh with a long tail.
Vav / Zayin / Nun sofit / YodVav is a plain single stroke; zayin has a bar that sticks out to the right at the top; final nun is an extra-long vav that drops below the line; yod is short and floats at the top.
He / Chet / TavHe has a gap (window) at the upper left; chet is closed; tav has a foot that points left.
Samekh / Mem sofitSamekh is rounded; final mem is square.
Tsadi / AyinTsadi's second stroke comes from the top right; ayin's second stroke comes from the top left.
Pe sofit / Tsadi sofitFinal pe curls inward at the top left; final tsadi forks upward like a Y.
Shin / SinShin has its dot on the upper right (sh); sin has its dot on the upper left (s). Dots are often left off in handwriting.
