# Per-video notes (captions read)

Status: research notes, 2026-11-03. I read English captions only (see `top100.md` for method and limits). Everything is in my own words. "Error" below means something the teacher says aloud that I judge misleading for a learner of Israeli or liturgical Hebrew; because captions are machine-made, a few of these could be caption damage, and I mark those "(caption doubt)".

## Alphabet and vowel courses (reading)

**HebrewPod101, "Learn ALL Hebrew Alphabet in 40 Minutes" (_UU6Fe7lqIo), 3.2M views.** A stitched compilation of a 20-lesson series. Letters go in alphabet order, two or so per lesson, but a vowel sign is added within the first few lessons so a real word can be spelled at once (father, roof, love). Each letter is shown in both print and handwriting, and each lesson ends with a short "insight" and a quiz. Errors: shva is called a silent vowel with no sound; the letter he is presented as something that "can also sound like a", which hides that a word-final he is only a spelling carrier for the vowel before it (caption doubt on the exact wording). Good: says plainly that modern text is written without vowel marks.

**HebrewPod101, "Learn Hebrew Writing #1" (JBVpQzvrJ4w), 1.6M views.** The first lesson of the same series. Right-to-left first, then the claim that every letter name begins with its own sound, then the two shapes of every letter (handwritten and print), and the first word. The name-gives-the-sound rule is a good memory hook but fails for several letters (ayin, tsadi, vav in its vowel role); it is stated without that caveat.

**Tim McNinch, "Hebrew Alphabet (Alefbet) + Vowels" (kkxvqIxTE3A), 1.4M views.** Blackboard teaching, one letter at a time in alphabet order, each with a stroke-by-stroke block script and two sample words. Calls out the silent carrier role of aleph and ayin, the dot toggles on bet, kaf and pe, and the five final forms. Ends with the alef-bet song. The vowel half is academic-style: each sign gets a picture mnemonic (a necktie for one, a nest of eggs for another), and signs come in long/short pairs. He says himself that native speakers simplify this, which is honest, but the learner is left with a seven-vowel system that Israeli speech does not use. Shva is deferred to "a whole lesson of its own". Error: long and short as audible differences (for Israeli Hebrew).

**Rabbi Stuart Federow, "Learn How to Read Hebrew in ONE HOUR" (tk1njVL723w), 1.3M views.** The most distinctive method in the set: a drilled call-and-response on a fixed beat. Letters are introduced in visually contrasted pairs with picture gimmicks (a gap versus no gap, a hole at the bottom versus the top, a "blasting cap" for the dotted plosives), and syllables are built at once by pairing each letter with the vowels taught so far. Final forms come late as "hinged" versions of the base letter. Shva is explained as a barely heard "uh" and given a useful rule: after a vowel, it ends the syllable. Errors: says Hebrew has no vowels; treats aleph and ayin as simply silent ("for our purposes"); gives chet two sounds in one lesson; says tav can be s in Ashkenazi but chooses Sephardi without teaching a switch. Strength: syllable reading from minute five.

**HebrewPod101, "Review Hebrew Alphabet in 16 minutes" (sEuRdbtQNYE), 754K views.** A fast pass over the alphabet then the dagesh and the vowel signs, introduced "a e i o" by sign. Repeats the claim that shva is silent. I saw no practice-word section in the captions.

**hebrewpodcasts, "Lesson 2 - Alphabet" (8jBoMImEpC0), 349K views.** Host and a second speaker go through the 22 letters in alphabet order, one animal word per letter. Notes that modern speech rarely tells aleph from ayin apart, that gimel with a mark makes a "j" sound for loanwords, and that shin has a right-dot and left-dot form. Names the five final forms in passing. Audio-led; reading practice is thin.

**The WORD in HEBREW, "Lesson 1: The Hebrew Alphabet" (Ekc3lIVdHEU), 504K views.** A short, clear lesson built around the "base sound" of each letter when it has no vowel, then a closing table of letters that share a sound in Israeli speech (aleph and ayin, bet-with-no-dot and vav, chaf-no-dot and chet, tet and tav, kaf-with-dot and kuf, sin and samech). That is the best treatment of merged sounds in the set. Resh is described as a gargle or Spanish r, giving learners a choice.

**Learn Hebrew with Daniel, "Hebrew Alphabet - Lesson 1" (KTSlu-ij_nU), 329K views.** Teacher from Tel Aviv, six short lessons, two or three letters at a time. Print and handwriting each time; vowel signs arrive with the first two letters, with the useful reassurance that two of the "a" signs sound the same so the learner need not tell them apart. Explains that vowel marks are mostly dropped in everyday text and that yud and vav double as vowels. Builds father/come sentences from the first two letters. Strong: reading-and-meaning from lesson one.

**Hebrew For Beginners, "The Hebrew Alphabet FREE course - Lesson 1" (M9t_hqFDB6E), 121K views.** Tiny three-or-four-letter lessons, in order, one quirk per letter. The rest of this channel's alphabet, vowel and handwriting series is covered in `docs/resources/youtube-hfb-playlists.md`.

**Hebrew Today, "Time to learn the Hebrew Nikud" (UbX1NrKvvHM), 277K views.** Frames the vowel marks as five sounds, each with several signs, and tells the learner flatly that the small differences between signs are no longer used, so any sign for a given sound is read the same. Demonstrates with one fixed carrier letter and swaps only the sign, which keeps the contrast clean. Shva gets one sentence (sometimes an e-like sound, sometimes nothing) with no rule. The reduced a-sign that is read as o in certain syllables is not mentioned. Fair for Israeli speech; thin for liturgical reading.

**Hebrew Today, "Let's Learn the Hebrew Alphabet, part 1" (jcb-xAvJKqE), 260K views.** The first five letters with an English-sound anchor for each, a food or animal word with a cultural aside, the bet/vet dot explained when bet appears, and a write-along. Says there are no vowel letters in Hebrew and introduces the vowel marks as a punctuation system, which skips the vowel role of vav and yud.

## Phrase and conversation videos

**HebrewPod101, "Learn Hebrew in 25 Minutes - ALL the Basics" (jvDCEXbxxL0), 3.3M views.** Stitched three-minute lessons: introductions, thanks, asking someone if they speak English. The teacher stops to show that a phrase changes with the gender of the speaker and listener, so most phrases have up to four forms. Claims there is no formal/informal split in Hebrew, which is a fair simplification for a beginner. Nothing on reading.

**HebrewPod101, "100 Phrases Every Hebrew Beginner Must-Know" (dKPKtvrXEfM), 885K views.** Phrase list by situation, then verbs. Spoken in Hebrew script plus transliteration on screen (the caption is auto-made, so I cannot confirm). Gender forms pointed out as they arise. No pronunciation drill.

**HebrewPod101, "Top 25 Must-Know Hebrew Phrases" (PgDtKfjXYc0), 627K views.** Uploader-supplied captions that include Hebrew script and Latin transliteration side by side. Gives a literal gloss for a common greeting (lit. "what is heard") and notes that the thanks word has one form only. Practical, no reading instruction; the transliteration is the only route to the sound.

## Prayer and Torah reading

**The WORD in HEBREW, "The Shema Prayer in Hebrew" (LCRIPntvZzs), 234K views.** The best-structured prayer video I read: right-to-left stated, then a key to the Latin transliteration (how vowels, ts, kh and r are spelled, a slash between syllables, capitals on the stressed syllable), then each word said slowly by syllable and again whole while a cursor follows the Hebrew. Tells the learner to watch the transliteration, so the Latin key, not the letters, carries the sound. Explains why the divine name is replaced by a substitute word when reading aloud. Stress is marked here and in almost no other video.

**BibleProject, "Shema - Listen" (6KQLOuIKaRA), 2.2M views.** A meaning-and-word-study video. No reading help for the prayer; it explains what the verb means in the Bible. Useful context for a gloss, not for reading skill.

**My Jewish Learning, "Shema: How to Say This Jewish Prayer" (vB58oyck-C8), 387K views.** A rabbi talks through when and how to say the prayer (hands over eyes, quiet second line). The auto-captions of the chanted part are unreadable, so I cannot judge the reading method. Ritual and gesture are covered; the letters are not taught.

**Rabbi Hajioff, "Practice reading the Shema" (pKQFMy9-6IE), 376K views.** Reads the first paragraph line by line for the learner to follow, with a note on the oversized ayin and dalet in the first line (together spelling "witness"). Call-and-follow reading with a book; no letter or vowel teaching.

**The YouTube Rabbi, "Mourner's Kaddish" (luk85AVuHCg), 597K views.** Captions hold about 13 words, so I could not read it. Listed as unusable.

**Jewish Learning and Prayer, "Aleinu" (Lf0ZDAvYInU), 238K views.** Captions hold about 14 words, so I could not read it. Listed as unusable.
